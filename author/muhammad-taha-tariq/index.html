What I do

I have worked in software quality since 2018: writing and executing test cases, building automation frameworks with Playwright and Selenium, and running performance tests with JMeter and Gatling. Most of that work has been on banking and financial systems, where a missed defect is expensive and a vague test is worse than no test.

That background shapes how I review testing tools. I read the vendor's own documentation and pricing page, check regulator and standards sources where a claim depends on them, and mark anything I could not verify. Where I have hands-on access to a tool, I say so. Where I do not, the review says that too.

What I cover

  • Test automation and QA workflows, including AI features inside testing tools.
  • Performance testing and the claims vendors make about speed and scale.
  • QA and software testing tools, judged on the tasks a team repeats.
  • Pricing and procurement: seat minimums, annual-only plans, and hidden add-ons.

How the work is produced

Every article goes through the same pipeline: research against primary sources, an evidence file with dated claims, a fact gate, an editorial review, and an automated preflight check before publishing. If a price or feature changes, the page is updated and the revision date moves.

I do not accept paid placements, and affiliate commissions never decide a verdict. If a tool is a poor fit, the review says so.

Corrections

If something is wrong, email hello@testverity.com with the page URL and the source that contradicts it. Substantive corrections are noted in the article's revision date.
